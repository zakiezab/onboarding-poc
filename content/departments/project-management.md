# Project Management

Source: Mobiz PM Onboarding Guide (MOBIZ PMO Process Guide v2.7).

<!-- spoken -->
Project Management owns every client engagement from the moment a deal
closes to the moment it's handed off to ongoing support. That's four stages:
intake, when a project is created and a PM assigned; initiation and
planning, when you build the project plan, risk log, and kickoff deck;
execution, running the project day to day against the agreed delivery
approach; and closure, wrapping up with a signed report and lessons learned.
Christian Rohrig, Director of PMO, sits above the team and is who
escalations reach if something can't be resolved at the PM Manager level.

You won't own a project on day one. Your first week is spent shadowing a
live project — sitting in on a status call, reviewing its risk log and
status report, and seeing how a project's Slack channel and folder structure
get set up at intake. From week two, you take ownership of your first
project's initiation and planning, with your manager reviewing alongside
you. By day ninety you're expected to run a full reporting cycle
independently and handle a change request start to finish.

The tools you'll live in day to day are ServiceNow for the project record
itself, SharePoint for documentation, and MS Project for scheduling.

## Reference

Not read aloud, but available when the new hire asks a question.

- **Two milestone types**: a Billing Milestone (defined by the SOW, triggers
  invoicing) and a Schedule Milestone (defined by the project plan, tracks
  delivery progress) — don't conflate them.
- **Time tracking**: every team member logs time weekly by Friday, reviewed
  daily. Missed entries escalate on a fixed clock — same day, then Friday
  reminder, then Monday, Tuesday, and by Wednesday the Director of PMO
  intervenes directly.
- **Change management**: a Change Request needs Director of PMO approval,
  then customer sponsor approval by email — not verbal — before a signed CR
  becomes a formal addendum to the SOW.
- **Reporting cadence**: Flash Reports for high-risk events, Weekly Status
  for tactical progress, an Executive Dashboard bi-weekly, a SteerCo Report
  monthly, and a Financial Health Report monthly.
- **Communication culture**: Slack first, short bullet-point emails, meetings
  capped at 25 minutes with 4 attendees, and every meeting produces an
  agenda, notes, and next steps with owners.
